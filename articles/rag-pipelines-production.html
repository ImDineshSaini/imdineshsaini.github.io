Everyone talks about RAG (Retrieval-Augmented Generation) — a method that gives AI answers based on your own documents — like it's magic. Embed your documents, search for relevant pieces, and generate an answer. Sounds easy.

I ran RAG in production for six months. It powered our KYC (Know Your Customer) — the process of verifying a customer's identity — compliance system. The theory is simple. The practice is full of edge cases and trade-offs that change your results.

Here's what nobody tells you about RAG until you're debugging production issues at 2 AM.

The Problem: Compliance Questions at Scale

Our digital banking platform onboards customers in 12 countries. Each country has different KYC and AML (Anti-Money Laundering) rules. Support teams kept asking things like: "Does this document meet India's KYC requirements?" or "What's the minimum age for an account in Germany?"

We had the answers spread across 2,000+ pages of regulatory documents. Finding them meant searching PDFs and cross-referencing rules. You had to hope you found the right section.

Enter RAG. The promise: ask questions in plain language and get accurate answers from our documents. The reality: it took three months to go from "sometimes works" to "actually reliable."

Lesson 1: Chunking is Harder Than It Looks

Our first version used naive chunking (splitting documents into smaller pieces) — every 500 tokens. It was simple and fast. It was also terrible.

We got answers like: "According to Section 4, customers must provide..." But Section 4 was about something else entirely. The real requirement was in Section 7.

The problem? Naive chunking breaks context.

Imagine splitting this sentence in the middle: "Customers under 18 years old [CHUNK BOUNDARY] are not eligible for premium accounts." Now one chunk says customers under 18 are fine. The other says they're not eligible. Both are technically correct, but neither tells the full story.

We fixed this with semantic chunking:

  • Respect document structure — don't split mid-sentence or mid-paragraph
  • Keep related content together, like rules with their exceptions
  • Add metadata (section headers, document title, country) to each chunk
  • Use overlapping chunks — the last 100 tokens of one chunk repeat at the start of the next

Retrieval accuracy jumped from 62% to 89% just from smarter chunking.

Lesson 2: Embeddings Are Not Created Equal

We started with OpenAI's text-embedding-ada-002. Embeddings (numerical representations of text that capture meaning) worked fine for general questions. They struggled with domain-specific terms.

"What is PEP screening?" returned chunks about "pepper screening" (a food safety term). We needed results about "Politically Exposed Person screening" (a KYC term).

Switching models didn't fix it. Instead, we fine-tuned our retrieval process:

  • Build a domain-specific glossary as a vector lookup table
  • Use hybrid search — combine vector similarity with keyword matching
  • Add query expansion — turn "PEP" into "Politically Exposed Person" before searching
  • Rerank results using a cross-encoder (a model that compares a question against each chunk directly)

Hybrid search was the game-changer. Pure vector search finds similar meanings but misses exact matches. Combining both gives you the best of both worlds.

Lesson 3: Retrieval Matters More Than Generation

Here's a surprising truth: 80% of RAG quality comes from retrieval, not generation. If you retrieve the wrong chunks, even GPT-4 can't save you. It will confidently answer based on irrelevant context.

We learned this the hard way. Our system told a support agent that German customers needed a "passport or driver's license" for KYC. That was technically true but incomplete. German regulations also accept national ID cards, which most Germans actually use.

Why did this happen? We only retrieved 3 chunks. The chunk about national ID cards ranked 4th and got left out.

The solution: retrieve more, rank better.

  • Retrieve top 10 chunks, not just top 3
  • Rerank them using a specialized cross-encoder
  • Pass top 5 reranked chunks to the LLM
  • Include a confidence score for each chunk
Python — RAG query with reranking 
async def query_rag(question: str, country: str) -> str:
    # Step 1: Retrieve top 10 chunks
    chunks = vector_db.search(
        query=question,
        filter={"country": country},
        top_k=10
    )

    # Step 2: Rerank with cross-encoder
    ranked = reranker.rerank(question, chunks, top_k=5)

    # Step 3: Check confidence threshold
    if ranked[0].score < 0.7:
        return "I don't have enough information to answer this."

    # Step 4: Generate answer from top chunks
    context = "\n\n".join([c.text for c in ranked])
    answer = await llm.complete(
        system="Answer using only the provided context.",
        user=f"Context:\n{context}\n\nQuestion: {question}"
    )
    return answer

This added 200ms to latency but cut incorrect answers by 60%.

Lesson 4: Metadata is Your Friend

Early on, we got questions like "What are the age requirements?" But age requirements vary by country and account type. Without context, the system returned a random answer — sometimes from the wrong country.

We fixed this by treating metadata as a first-class citizen:

  • Document metadata: country, regulation type, last updated date
  • Chunk metadata: section header, page number, document title
  • Query metadata: user's role, customer's country, account type

Now when someone asks "What are the age requirements?", the system knows they're helping a German customer. It filters chunks by country before searching. This is RAG 2.0: context-aware retrieval.

Lesson 5: Monitoring is Non-Negotiable

In production, things drift. Documents get updated. Regulations change. User questions evolve.

We monitor four key areas:

  • Retrieval metrics: How often do retrieved chunks contain the answer? We sample 10% of queries for manual review.
  • Confidence scores: Low confidence answers get flagged for human review.
  • Fallback rate: How often does the system say "I don't know"?
  • User feedback: Thumbs up or down on every answer.

When retrieval accuracy drops below 85%, it usually means new documents were added without re-embedding the collection.

Lesson 6: Know When to Say "I Don't Know"

The most dangerous thing an AI system can do is confidently give a wrong answer. In banking, that's unacceptable.

We explicitly tell our LLM:

"If the retrieved context doesn't contain enough information to answer accurately, say 'I don't have enough information to answer this question.' Do NOT make up answers."

We also enforce confidence thresholds. If the top-ranked chunk scores below 0.7, we return "Not enough information found" instead of generating an answer.

False negatives (saying "I don't know" when we could answer) are annoying. False positives (giving wrong answers with confidence) are dangerous.

What We'd Do Differently

If I built this system again from scratch, I'd change five things:

  • Start with hybrid search, not pure vector search
  • Invest in evaluation early — build a test set of questions with known answers
  • Use a reranker from day one, not as an afterthought
  • Add human-in-the-loop feedback from the start — let users mark wrong answers
  • Build for observability — log everything, you'll need it for debugging

   - [ ] Human feedback loop (thumbs up/down)

The Reality Check

RAG isn't magic. It's a powerful pattern that needs careful engineering to work in production.

The good news? When it works, it changes everything. Our support team answers compliance questions in seconds, not minutes. Accuracy is above 90%. The system keeps learning from feedback.

Getting there took work. If you're building RAG systems, plan time for iteration. Your first version won't be production-ready. That's okay.

"RAG isn't plug-and-play. It's a framework that needs tuning, monitoring, and continuous improvement."

Key Takeaways:

  • Chunking strategy has a huge impact on retrieval quality
  • Hybrid search (vector + keyword) beats pure vector search
  • 80% of quality comes from retrieval, not generation
  • Metadata makes retrieval context-aware and accurate
  • Monitor retrieval metrics, not just generation quality
  • Embrace "I don't know" — it's better than being confidently wrong

RAG takes work to get right. But when it works, it's powerful. Our team now answers compliance questions in seconds, not minutes.
